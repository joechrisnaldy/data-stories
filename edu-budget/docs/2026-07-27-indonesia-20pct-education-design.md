## Thesis (chosen)

> Indonesia wrote an education spending share into its constitution, fought for it in court, and has
> met it every year since 2009. The money is guaranteed. The learning is not. A percentage is a
> promise about an input, and any promise written that way can be kept in full while the thing it was
> meant to buy stays out of reach.

## Brainstorm decisions (locked)

- **Thesis:** you can mandate the money, not the learning.

## The verified spine (every figure has a primary-source quote on file)

### 1. The mandate, and the fight to enforce it
- UUD 1945 Art. 31(4), Fourth Amendment, adopted **10 August 2002**: education gets
  **"sekurang-kurangnya dua puluh persen"** (at least 20%) of the APBN and of each APBD.
- The Constitutional Court heard APBN challenges **four times from 2005** before the decisive ones.
- **Putusan 026/PUU-IV/2006** (pronounced 1 May 2007): the 2007 budget's education ceiling of
  **11.8%** (Rp54.07 trillion) held unconstitutional. The court noted no budget from FY2004 to FY2007
  had ever reached 20%.
- **Putusan 13/PUU-VI/2008** (13 August 2008): the **entire** revised 2008 budget law held
  unconstitutional because education was only **15.6%**; the court found **"kesengajaan"**, a
  deliberate violation, and ordered compliance **by the FY2009 budget at the latest**.
- Kemenkeu confirms the 20% mandate has been met **since 2009**.
- Recent compliance: **2024 Rp665.0T = 20.00%**; **2025 Rp724.3T = 20.00%**; **2026 Rp769.1T**.

### 2. Twenty percent of a small pie
- Total state spending is only about **15% of GDP** (2024: belanja negara 15.02% of GDP), so
  20% of it is **3.00% of GDP** (2024: Rp665.0T of Rp22,139.0T GDP). Cross-checked on APBN 2025:
  20.00% of belanja = **2.98% of GDP**.
- Why the base is small: **tax-to-GDP 12.0%** (2023) vs **Asia-Pacific 19.5%** and **OECD 33.9%**,
  and essentially unchanged in 16 years (12.2% in 2007).
- Peers, education spending as % of GDP: **Philippines 3.93%** (2024), **Malaysia 3.51%** (2023),
  **Indonesia 3.00%** (2024, budget-based), **Viet Nam 2.89%** (2022), **Thailand 2.52%** (2023),
  **OECD members 5.06%** (2022).
- Subnational compliance is patchy: **22% of districts/cities and 35% of provinces** did not meet the
  same 20% rule (World Bank, 2020).

### 3. What the money genuinely bought (the honest counterpoint)
- Upper-secondary net enrolment **40.1% (2001) to 81.5% (2018)**; 56.6% in 2009.
- Lower-secondary net enrolment **73.7% (2001) to 94.9% (2023)**.
- Secondary gross enrolment **55.6% (2000) to 96.0% (2023)**.
- Adult literacy **90.4% (2004) to 96.0% (2020)**; mean years of schooling **7.57 (2006) to 8.56 (2020)**.
- PISA coverage of Indonesia's 15-year-olds rose from **46% (2001) to 85% (2018)**, and more than
  **1.1 million** additional 15-year-olds entered PISA-eligible schooling between 2012 and 2022.

### 4. The learning gap, handled honestly
- PISA 2022: **maths 366, reading 359, science 383** (OECD averages 472 / 476 / 485).
- OECD's own summary: 2022 is **on par with 2003** (reading, maths) and **2006** (science). Earlier
  gains, including the 2009 reading peak of 402, were given back.
- Baseline proficiency (Level 2) in 2022: **maths 18%, reading 25%, science 34%**
  (OECD averages 69 / 74 / 76). Reading below-baseline rose **19 pp** from 2012 to 2022.
- **World Bank learning poverty: 53%** of children at late primary age not proficient in reading
  (April 2024 brief; underlying data TIMSS 2015 / ANER 2014), 18 pp worse than East Asia and Pacific.
- **CRITICAL HONESTY CAVEATS, both must appear:**
  1. **COVID:** OECD-average scores fell about 10 points in reading and nearly 15 in maths between
     2018 and 2022. OECD says the fall is "only partially attributable" to COVID and found no clear
     link to closure length. 60% of Indonesian students reported closures over three months.
  2. **Enrolment expansion mechanically drags the mean.** OECD names Indonesia a rapid-expansion
     country and frames the decline as "linked to integrating marginalised students, not system
     deterioration." **OECD's own enrolment-adjusted re-reading of 2012 to 2022: science up, reading
     down, maths stable.** The naive "spending up, scores down" narrative is NOT supportable and must
     not be written.
- Comparability rule: trends valid only from **reading 2000, maths 2003, science 2006**.

### 5. Where the money actually goes
- **Indonesia never reached "enough."** OECD PISA 2022: above **USD 75,000** cumulative spending per
  student (ages 6 to 15) more spending stops predicting higher scores. Indonesia spends about
  **USD 19,700**, far below that threshold. So this is NOT a case of money being proven useless; it is
  a case of a guaranteed share never adding up to a sufficient amount.
- World Bank WDR 2018: the cross-country spending-learning link is **"weak and statistically
  insignificant after controlling for income per capita."** Indonesia-specific: district education
  spending vs exam results shows a very weak link.
- Salaries dominate. In the 2026 draft, **Rp274.7T of Rp757.8T (36.2%)** of the education budget goes
  to educators' pay, including **Rp69T** in professional allowances for 1.6 million regional civil
  servant teachers and **Rp19.2T** for 754,747 non-civil-servant teachers.
- The strongest causal evidence: a **randomised experiment** doubling Indonesian teachers' base pay
  produced **no improvement in student learning** (de Ree, Muralidharan, Pradhan & Rogers, 2018).
  WDR 2018 reaches the same verdict on the certification allowance.
- **The category is elastic.** In the 2026 draft budget, **Rp223T of the Rp757.8T education budget
  (29.4%)** was the free nutritious meals programme (MBG) for 82.9 million beneficiaries. Total MBG
  2026 allocation Rp335T, later cut to Rp268T. **Report existence, timing and amounts ONLY. No
  political framing, no verdict on the programme.**

## Guardrails

- **Objective and descriptive. NO politics.** No verdict on any government, party, minister, or on the
  meals programme. Existence, timing, amounts, and sourced evaluations only.
- **Do NOT write "spending rose, scores fell" as a causal claim.** OECD's enrolment-adjusted reading
  and the COVID confounder must both appear wherever the PISA decline is discussed.
- **Do NOT claim money does not matter.** Indonesia is far below the spending threshold; the evidence
  says the share never became a sufficient amount, not that spending is futile.
- No em or en dashes; APA 7 references for every external fact; verified or omit.
- The child in the opening is an illustrative composite drawn from the learning-poverty statistic, not
  a real individual, and must be flagged as such.
- Mixed-year comparisons must be labelled (Indonesia's 3.00% is 2024 budget-based; peers are
  World Bank/UIS 2022 to 2024). The World Bank/UIS Indonesia series is NOT usable at face value for
  recent years (it reports 1.28% of GDP), so the Kemenkeu-derived figure is used and the discrepancy
  disclosed.

   real data, realistic correlations, habits explain only R^2 0.16. Available for a future post.
