# Post 28 design: JHT and the 401(k), pre-registration

Written 2026-10-21 (New York time), before any result is computed. Later changes are added as dated amendments
at the end, each with its reason.

## 1. Question and stance (the owner's)

The 401(k) is not compulsory, yet it works because savers watch their money grow. Indonesia already makes workers
save through BPJS Ketenagakerjaan's Jaminan Hari Tua (JHT), with employer and worker contributions, but a worker can
pay in for years without knowing. Indonesia does not need a 401(k); it needs JHT to feel like one.

Reader: Indonesian workers. Opening: the owner's own story (his 401(k) in the US; not knowing about JHT at home),
using only facts he supplies. Not financial advice: no sentence tells a reader to buy, sell, move or hold anything.

## 2. Owner decisions so far (2026-10-21)

- Main evidence: growth over a career. Each saver in their own currency, after their own inflation.
- The 401(k) side has two lines: an S&P 500 fund and a target-date-style mix.
- Scope: growth; what goes in (contributions, match, tax break); Singapore; what the saver can see.
- Malaysia is dropped: its EPF site blocks Anthropic's crawler.
- Singapore comes only from CPF Board datasets on data.gov.sg (Singapore Open Data Licence).
- Awareness: the owner's story plus measured coverage; no claim about how many workers do not know.
- Window: 2005 to 2020, the years with a documented JHT rate for member balances.
- JHT bonuses (the 2008 and 2009 incentives, the two 2013 one-off payouts) are included; the base-rate-only result is
  reported beside it.
- Working title direction: the contrast ("Forced to Save, Never Shown the Balance").

## 3. Data

- **JHT rate, 2005 to 2020.** The rate PT Jamsostek / BPJS Ketenagakerjaan set for member balances each year, from the
  audited notes in its annual reports (2005 to 2014, 2016 to 2020). 2015 had no single annual decree; its figure is
  the year-end rate BPJS itself reported for 2015, labelled as such. Every figure is extracted by script from the
  stored report, with its quote checked (one manifest row per figure).
- **JHT investment yield, 2016 to 2025**, from the same reports: what BPJS publishes about the fund, as opposed to
  what members are credited.
- **Contributions.** JHT: PP 46/2015 Pasal 16. JP: PP 45/2015 Pasal 28. 401(k): Vanguard, How America Saves 2026
  (Vanguard-recordkept plans only, which the post says); IRS for the rules. CPF: data.gov.sg contribution rates.
- **US returns.** Aswath Damodaran, Historical Returns on Stocks, Bonds and Bills, 1928 to 2025: S&P 500 with
  dividends and the 10-year US Treasury bond.
- **Prices.** IMF Consumer Price Index, headline, Indonesia and the United States (the licence cleared in Post 27).
- **Glide path.** Vanguard's published target-date glide path and the Target Retirement funds' prospectus.
- **Coverage (prose).** BPJS's own count of active JHT members; ILO modelled estimates of employees.
- Not used: FRED (its terms bar use with AI), bps.go.id and kwsp.gov.my (both block Anthropic's crawler),
  peraturan.bpk.go.id (a bot challenge). Vanguard's terms license content for personal and noncommercial use, so the
  post cites its facts and reproduces none of its charts.

## 4. Rules fixed before any result

- **R1 Window.** Calendar years 2005 to 2020 for every growth line.
- **R2 The saver.** Each year the saver puts in the same real amount: one unit, raised each year by that country's
  annual-average CPI. Contributions arrive mid-year. Each line follows JHT's own crediting rule: the year's rate on
  the opening balance plus half the rate on the year's contributions.
- **R3 JHT line.** The year's rate from R1's data. Bonuses: the 2008 incentive is 2.1% of the end-2007 balance; the
  2009 incentive of 1.60% is added to that year's rate; the 2013 payouts of 6.50% and 0.81% are paid on the opening
  2013 balance. The check line uses the base rates only.
- **R4 US lines.** (a) S&P 500 with dividends. (b) A target-date-style mix for a saver aged 25 in 2005: Vanguard's glide
  path holds 90% stocks from age 20 to 40, so the mix is 90% S&P 500 and 10% 10-year Treasury bond, rebalanced each
  year. Both lines are charged 0.08% a year, the expense ratio of Vanguard's Target Retirement funds. The mix is a
  simplification: Vanguard's funds also hold international stocks and bonds, and the post says so.
- **R5 Real terms.** Each balance is valued in that country's prices of December 2020 (IMF CPI). The result for each
  line is its final real balance divided by the real total put in.
- **R6 Year by year.** JHT's real rate each year is (1 + rate) / (1 + December-to-December inflation) minus 1.
- **R7 What goes in.** Shares of wages, worker and employer shown separately: JHT; JP (shown apart, because it is a
  pension, not a savings account); a typical 401(k) from How America Saves 2026; CPF for workers aged 55 and under
  (highest wage band), in force on 1 January 2026.
- **R8 What the saver is shown.** For each year 2014 to 2025: whether BPJS published the rate credited to JHT
  balances, and the fund's published investment yield.

## 5. Charts (four)

1. Growth 2005 to 2020: the real value of the same yearly saving: JHT (with the base-only line), S&P 500, the 90/10 mix.
2. JHT year by year: the credited rate against Indonesian inflation, 2005 to 2020.
3. What goes in: worker and employer shares of wages, JHT, JP, 401(k), CPF.
4. What the saver is shown: JHT's published investment yield 2014 to 2025, and the credited rate where BPJS published it.

## 6. Predictions (written before any result)

- P1. Over 2005 to 2020, the S&P 500 line ends highest, the 90/10 mix second, JHT third, in real terms.
- P2. The JHT line still ends above what was put in (its real result is above 1).
- P3. JHT's real rate is negative in at least one year, most likely 2005 or 2008, when inflation was high.
- P4. Including the bonuses moves JHT's result by less than its gap to the 90/10 mix.
- P5. JHT's worker plus employer share (5.7%) is below a typical 401(k)'s combined rate in How America Saves.

## 7. Limits stated in the post

- Different currencies and risks: an S&P 500 fund can fall sharply in a single year, while the law sets a floor under
  JHT's credited rate (PP 46/2015 Pasal 27(3)). The comparison is about what each saver can see grow, not about
  which to choose.
- 2021 to 2025 are missing because BPJS has not published the credited rate since 2020.
- How America Saves covers Vanguard's clients only.

## 8. Amendments

**A1 (2026-10-22, New York time; after the data build, before any result).** No result of R1 to R8 has been computed.
- **Sources of the 2016 to 2020 rates.** Section 3 said the rates come from audited notes. For 2016 to 2020 the notes in
  the stored reports either are absent from the text layer or list only monthly claim rates, so these five rates come
  from the reports' narrative sections, each quoted in `docs/jht-manifest.csv`. For 2018 the post uses 6.26%, the
  year-end rate the report gives, not 6.23%, the last monthly claim rate in its notes.
- **2015 and 2020.** 2015 is the year-end rate BPJS reported (its release of 19 February 2016), as section 3 said.
  Reading 2020's 5.59% as the rate for balances is an inference from the report's wording; the post says so.
- **Yield window.** Section 3 said 2016 to 2025; R8 and the data use 2014 to 2025. 2017 has no yield in any
  text-layer document and is shown as missing.
- **Crediting.** JHT credited contributions by days held in some years and by time-weighted balances from 2015.
  R2's mid-year rule is a simplification applied the same way to every line; the post says so.
- **Bonuses.** No document states which balance the 2009 incentive and the 2013 payouts applied to; R3's readings are
  inferences and the post says so. The base-only line is unaffected.
- **Prices (owner decision).** The IMF's Indonesian index differs from BPS's published December inflation in 2008,
  2013 and 2020. R5 and R6 keep the IMF index; chart 2 and the text also show BPS's published December rate, from Bank
  Indonesia's Data Inflasi (the file used in Post 27), and say where the two differ.
- **US returns (owner decision).** Damodaran asks that his data not be used in public policy debates (a request, not a
  licence term). The post uses it only to show what a 401(k) saver saw, and credits him and his inputs.

**A2 (2026-10-22, New York time; before any result).** No result of R1 to R8 has been computed.
- **How America Saves' year.** R7 takes the typical 401(k) from How America Saves 2026, which tables 2021 to 2025.
  Its 2025 participation and deferral figures are estimates (linear extrapolation and judgement, its methodology
  says), so R7 uses 2024, the latest year that is not an estimate. The employer part is the average total
  contribution rate less the average deferral rate, both for 2024; the post says it is a difference of averages.
- **The fee.** The 0.08% of R4 is the expense ratio printed in the January 2026 prospectus; no stored document gives
  the funds' expense ratios for 2005 to 2020, and the post says so.

**A3 (2026-10-22, New York time; AFTER the first results, stated as such).** Two independent recomputations from
this document and the raw files reproduced every result except one, which the rules left open: R4 charges the
0.08% fee without saying how. The build charges it on the year's closing value; both recomputations subtracted it
from the year's return. The two readings differ in the third decimal of the US multiples (the 90/10 mix is 2.28
one way and 2.29 the other) and change no prediction. The build keeps its reading and records the other as a check
in `results.json`; the post prints the multiples to one decimal, which both readings give. Also recorded: R6 and
chart 2 use the base rate in 2009, without the incentive, as the chart's note says.
