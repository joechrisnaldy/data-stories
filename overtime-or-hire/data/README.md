# Data for this post

The survey file stays out of the repository. Download the American Time Use Survey multi-year activity summary file, `atussum-0324.zip`, from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (`https://www.bls.gov/tus/data/datafiles-0324.htm`) into this folder. `profile_data.py` checks it against the hash it records and stops if the file differs. The copy used here came from an Internet Archive capture of `https://www.bls.gov/tus/datafiles/atussum-0324.zip`; the site refused automated downloads.

The sample: respondents employed and at work, aged 18 to 64, with diaries from 2015 to 2024, 45,081 people. Left out: 2,337 people whose usual hours vary and 5 with no usual hours. Estimates use the survey's final weight, and its pandemic weight for the year that needs it, as the data dictionary says. With those weights, the diaries of 2024 reproduce the published figure for sleep, 9.04 hours a day.

The other sources are documents, stored in `sources/` (also out of the repository) in the folders `store_text.py` names. `docs/sources.json` gives the address of each document the post quotes: the Bureau of Labor Statistics releases, the Department of Labor fact sheets, Pencavel's discussion paper, the RIETI discussion paper, the RAND report, the Center for American Progress report and Indonesia's labour law. `store_text.py` also converts the documents the provenance audit quotes: the survey's coding lexicon (`https://www.bls.gov/tus/lexicons/lexiconnoex0324.pdf`) and data dictionary (`https://www.bls.gov/tus/dictionaries/atusintcodebk0324.pdf`), the regulation on exemptions (`https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-V/subchapter-A/part-541`) and Indonesia's earlier regulation on working time and overtime (`https://peraturan.bpk.go.id/Download/154582/PP%20Nomor%2035%20Tahun%202021.pdf`). Store each in its folder, then run `python3 store_text.py`.
