**How FC2's mean-return test differs from the papers' regression tests.**

1. **What is measured.** Both papers regress gold's return on the stock return and test the slope (co-movement) on extreme days. FC2 tests the asset's mean return on those days (its level). These can disagree. An asset with strong upward drift can average a gain on crash days while still falling hardest on the worst of them; a slope test discards the drift through the intercept, but a mean-return test keeps it. The post should call FC2 a test of whether the asset held or gained value on the worst days. That matches the value wording in Q3 and Q10, not the correlation wording in Q4 and Q8.
2. **The weak threshold.** Baur and McDermott count a non-positive slope as weak (Q12), and Baur and Lucey require b1 + b2 to be at most zero as a point estimate (Q6); neither paper states a significance rule for the weak case. FC2's weak test, "not significantly below zero", is the design's own choice. It is lenient for a volatile asset, because a wide interval stops rejection: Bitcoin can pass "weak" because its returns are noisy.
3. **The strong threshold.** Baur and McDermott require a slope that is negative and significant (Q13). FC2 requires a mean return that is significantly above zero. The sign flips because one is a slope and the other a level. Same idea, different statistic.
4. **Model and errors.** Baur and Lucey use OLS with lags and Newey-West errors (5 lags). Baur and McDermott use one GARCH(1,1) regression on all days, estimated by maximum likelihood, in which the extreme-day terms add to the average slope. FC2 is a t test on the subsample of worst days only, with no hedge term and no heteroskedasticity correction.
5. **Cut-offs.** Baur and Lucey use 5%, 2.5% and 1%; Baur and McDermott use 10%, 5% and 1%, with nested dummies whose totals are cumulative (p. 13). FC2's daily 5% and 1% and monthly 10% cut-offs all appear in Baur and McDermott. The monthly 20% robustness check is in neither paper.
6. **Timing.** FC2 uses same-day returns, as both papers do for the contemporaneous effect. Baur and Lucey find the effect fades within about 15 trading days (Q14). FC2's two-day robustness check stays inside that range.

**What does not support the design's wording.**

- **Weak and strong safe haven do not come from Baur and Lucey.** They come from Baur and McDermott, who say they are extending Baur and Lucey (Q9). Section 4 and 8.2 (FC2) must cite Baur and McDermott for these terms.
- **"Or negatively correlated" cannot be checked in the 2010 version of record.** The design's section 2 cites "Baur and Lucey (2010)", but the published abstract defines both terms as "uncorrelated" (Q4), and so does the 2009 SSRN abstract. The wording "uncorrelated or negatively correlated" is in the 2006 working paper (Q1, Q2), and as strong and weak in Baur and McDermott (Q7, Q8). The post should cite the version that holds the words it uses, or say "uncorrelated with stocks on average / in a crash" and cite the 2010 article.
- **Section 2's definitions are about correlation, but FC2 tests mean returns.** The design calls FC2 a simplified version of the papers' test. That is fair only if the post says that the papers test co-movement (slopes) and FC2 tests whether value was kept (levels), and cites Q3 or Q10 for the value-based reading.
- **FC2's weak bar, "not significantly below zero", is the design's own operationalisation.** Neither paper states it. Present it as the post's rule, not as the literature's.
- **The "15 trading days" figure is from the 2006 working paper.** The published abstract and the 2009 SSRN abstract say only that the effect is short-lived (the SSRN abstract adds that buying gold one day after a shock loses money). Whether the published article keeps 15 days was not checked. If the post uses the number, cite the working paper.
- **The Baur and Lucey working paper is not internally consistent.** Its Table 1 writes the hedge condition as b1 < 0 (strictly negative, a strong hedge in Baur and McDermott's terms), while its text says uncorrelated or negatively correlated. Its text on p. 13 also gives the 2.5% and 1% results as tables 4 and 5, but the captions number them 5 and 6. Neither affects the post, but quote the text, not Table 1.

### Where the sources do not support the design's wording

1. **S&P 500 permission (8.4) is a judgement call, not a grant.** The S&P notice on FRED says "Reproduction of S&P 500 in any form is prohibited" without S&P's prior written permission (line 204). FRED section III allows pre-approval series to be used only for non-commercial educational or personal use (D8). But FRED's own FAQ 3 (line 129) and the full Terms of Use (line 271) say that anything beyond your own personal use needs the data owner's permission. No stored text says that rebasing to 100, or showing only returns or drawdowns, takes the post outside S&P's notice. "No raw daily levels" is the owner's mitigation; it is not a permission. FRED does say it cannot give permission on S&P's behalf. The stored notice names an address for permission requests, if the owner wants one.
2. **"3 to 4 hours after the S&P 500 close" (8.1, 8.2) holds only on full trading days.** It is arithmetic from D4 (4 PM ET) and D10 (00:00 UTC). FRED also says the market "sometimes closes early" on holidays, and on those days the gap is longer.
3. **"Fixed at 00:00 UTC" (8.1) is a fixing time, not a single trade.** The CM Prices overview (line 146) says daily reference rates are time-weighted over an observation window. None of the stored pages gives the window's length. The Price page sends readers to "frequently asked questions below" for the timestamp convention, but the stored page has no such FAQ. The "following day" wording therefore rests on the API reference data alone (D10).
4. **"Multi-exchange" (8.1)** paraphrases "constituent markets" (D13). The stored overview is current documentation (it shows "Last updated 2 months ago"), and nothing stored says the method was the same for every year from 2010 to 2026.
5. **Coin Metrics GitHub status.** The README says "The archives are updated daily." (line 18), but the repository's last commit was on 2026-05-24 and the CSV's last PriceUSD date is 2026-05-23. That supports the design's "ends on 23 May 2026", not the README. The design's statement that Coin Metrics' former terms "named [the GitHub CSV] as permitted" cannot be checked from any stored document: those terms are no longer online and the README does not mention them. The stored `generate.js` is the current version. The 2,366-day match covers 2019-12-01 onward; I did not check older versions of the script.
6. **Gold: "calendar-month" (8.1)** is not in the workbook's wording, which says only "monthly prices" and "average of daily rates". It is a fair reading but an inference. **CC BY 4.0 for the Pink Sheet** depends on the qualifier "Unless specifically labeled otherwise" (D21). No sheet in the workbook carries any licence or restriction label (I searched every sheet). The terms add "Exceptions for Some Third-Party Data" (line 232), and the Gold row lists third-party sources (cell T103: Bloomberg Finance L.P., Kitco.com, London Bullion Market, Thomson Reuters Datastream and others). So CC BY 4.0 rests on the absence of a label, and the post should also use the World Bank's own credit format (D22). The terms page is dated 23 March 2018.
7. **WHO field definitions are not on WHO's current pages.** `New_cases` and `Date_reported` are defined only on the archived 2023 page (D26, D27). That page described the file then served at `covid19.who.int/WHO-COVID-19-global-data.csv`, which was daily at the time. Today the file name `WHO-COVID-19-global-data.csv` is WHO's weekly file, and the daily file is `WHO-COVID-19-global-daily-data.csv`. The column names match, but the post should cite the current pages (D23 to D25) for meaning, and the archive only for the field wording. The same archived page also says WHO's daily counts close at 23:59 CET/CEST, so a WHO "day" is a Central European day, not a UTC day.
8. **"Countries' reports reach WHO up to two days late" (8.3) is not supported** by any stored WHO page, current or archived. They speak only of unquantified "reporting delays" and "variable delays". Unless the figure comes from the author's own comparison (and is labelled as such), drop it.
9. **"WHO's reporting weeks (Monday to Sunday)" (8.3) is inferred, not stated.** WHO labels its aggregates "weekly data to week end date" (cases page, line 616) and refers to "the week commencing on 11 September 2023", which was a Monday. The vet's copy of the weekly file has every `Date_reported` on a Sunday (84,240 rows). That is consistent with weeks running Monday to Sunday, but no stored WHO sentence says it.
10. **"A group of European countries report only on Sundays" and "France is absent" (8.1, 8.3)** are vet findings from the data, not WHO statements. D28 and D29 support the general point only. Those statements also describe current reporting practice ("As of 25 August 2023 ..."), not 2020 specifically.
11. **"WHO's name used nowhere else" (8.4) conflicts with 8.3,** which requires the post to say France is absent "from WHO's counts" and that reports reach WHO late. The terms bar use of the name without prior written approval (D33), while requiring attribution as the metadata indicates (D32). Either word those sentences without the name (for example "the source's counts"), or decide that naming the source in a factual description is part of attribution. Owner decision.
12. **Coin Metrics credit (8.4)** names a licence link and "transformed by the author". The deed's credit also asks for a link to the material and, if supplied, a disclaimer notice (D37). The README supplies one (provided "without any warranty"), and it gives no copyright notice. Add a link to the GitHub repository.
13. Checked and not triggered: the FRED terms also bar using FRED content in the development or training of machine-learning or AI systems (section II). Analysing the data for a post is neither, so this does not apply to the design as written.
